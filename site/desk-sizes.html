Space planning / Desk dimensions

100 cm or 120 cm: which desk fits your day?

The extra 20 cm is easy to overlook on a product page. In a small room, it can be the difference between space for a notebook and room to open a cupboard. Start with your equipment and the movement space around it.

Compare like with like

Two desks can have the same width and very different depths. A 100 × 60 cm surface has an area of 0.60 m². A 120 × 60 cm surface is 0.72 m²: 20% more surface area, calculated from those example dimensions. A 120 × 50 cm desk has the same area as the first example, but a different shape. Area alone does not tell you whether your screen and keyboard will fit.

Try a paper layout before ordering

  1. Mark the proposed footprint on the floor with paper or a material that will not damage your flooring.
  2. Lay out your equipment on an existing table within the proposed width and depth.
  3. Put your chair in position and check the space needed to sit down and move away.
  4. Open nearby doors and drawers. Look for anything the desk or chair would obstruct.
  5. Check cables can reach sockets without stretching or crossing a walkway.

Look beneath the top

A drawer unit or support rail can reduce usable legroom. Compare the clear underside dimensions and the chair's armrest height. The nominal desk height does not describe the available knee space.

Real sizes are not always round numbers

For example, IKEA lists a MICKE variant at 105 × 50 cm and a LAGKAPTEN / ADILS variant at 120 × 60 cm. Use the exact product dimensions rather than treating every compact desk as 100 cm wide. Retailer listings checked 5 November 2026; confirm the current variant before ordering.

Our recommendation is a method: choose the smallest footprint that accommodates your actual equipment, comfortable use and clear access. We have not tested the linked products. These ordinary retailer links earn us no commission.
